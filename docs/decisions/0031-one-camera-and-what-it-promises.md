# 0031. One camera, and what starting it promised

Status: Accepted

**Rewritten by [#152](https://github.com/vik8174/word-crossword-game/issues/152). There is no camera any more.** This record used to describe one rectangle of a painted world, 3200 by 1400, moving between four points of it. That world is gone: `apps/web/src/garden/` no longer has a `camera.ts`, a `world.ts`, or a `paint-*` module that draws anything but the greeting cloth. What replaced it, what promise the camera made that still has to be kept, and what the original record got right and wrong, are below. The history of how the camera worked is kept at the end, in its own section, because a decision that vanished leaves the next reader wondering whether it was ever made.

## Decision

**A scene is a picture, not a place.** Three photographs — the gate, the doors, the hall — stand in for the one continuous painted world `#115` gave the app. `GardenScene` (`garden/GardenScene.tsx`) shows exactly one of them at a time, chosen by `SceneId` (`garden/garden-controls.ts`). The three pictures were generated together and accepted as a set specifically so that the gate frames the temple, and the doors frame the same interior the hall shows head-on — the content chain that used to be the camera flying through a doorway is now baked into the artwork itself (`handoffs/scenes/README.md`).

**The transition was instant here, and that was a scope boundary rather than a design decision — [#153](https://github.com/vik8174/word-crossword-game/issues/153) is the ticket that filled it in.** What this ticket promised was only that removing the camera left the application working: a screen change and a scene change happen in the same render, one picture is simply not the other one any more, and nothing between them stutters because nothing between them exists yet. That promise is kept unchanged by #153's own crossfade — an 1800ms fade, a 26-second forward push, a warm bloom and the temple's own red opening out of the middle, all carried in from the scene prototype this record used to point at and now built in `garden/scene-transition.ts` — because the transition runs on a change of _scene_, not on the render a screen change causes; two screens standing on one picture still change in the same render, with nothing to travel between them.

**Which picture a screen stands in front of is a total function of the screen, with no `null` case.** The old `locationFor` answered `null` for `connecting` and `unavailable` — "wherever the garden already stood" — because both were reachable from more than one place in a continuous world and remembering where the camera last was mattered. There is no camera to remember a position for now, so both screens get a picture of their own: `doors`, the temple's threshold, which is where a visitor who has not yet been let in or told no stands (`handoffs/scenes/README.md`; `garden/use-room-garden.ts`'s `sceneFor`). A fifth screen added to `RoomScreen` and not placed in `sceneFor` fails to compile, for the same reason the old switch had no `default`.

**A game ending is still not a journey, and it is now trivially true rather than an arithmetic coincidence.** `playing`, `closed-early` and `finished` all map to `hall` — the same picture, not merely the same point of a shared world — so there is nothing for the background to do when a game ends. The old record proved this from `isSamePlace(HALL, CONGRATULATIONS)`; the new one does not need a proof, because showing the same `<img>` twice is not an event.

**The petal layer no longer culls against a doorway, and `airFor` had to be corrected because of it.** The old rule — "a petal whose place falls inside the doorway is not drawn" — was geometry that made sense against one continuous world where the interior was visible through a window whose position the camera reported every frame (`world.ts`'s `openingOnScreen`). There is no such window to report a position for now: the doors picture's own glimpse of the hall is a painted detail, not a live viewport onto anything, so a petal falling in front of it is simply a petal falling in front of a picture. The coarser rule this one sat inside — petals fade out entirely for the three screens a finished or ended game stands on — now has to do the whole of that job alone, and `room-air.ts`'s `airFor` had one of the three wrong: `finished` answered `petals`, which was safe only because the removed culling drew nothing indoors regardless of what `airFor` said. Once the culling was gone that safety net went with it, and the stale answer let petals fall inside the temple on the one screen this record has always said gets none — caught after this ticket first shipped, and fixed here alongside it. `playing`, `closed-early` and `finished` all answer `still` now. The fine-grained cutout bought realism for a screen — the doors — where it turns out not to be missed; re-adding it against the real artwork, if it ever is, needs its own measurement against real pixels, the way `scenes/gate-chrome.ts` replaced a formula with one (see "What #151 already found", below).

**A phone still changes nothing about the scene; `prefers-reduced-motion` does again, now that #153 gave it something to govern.** The camera was the thing a phone's narrow window and a reader's reduced-motion setting were both answered for, and removing it left neither with anything to switch off — true for a phone still, since no width rule gates the crossfade either. `prefers-reduced-motion` is different: it is the fourth thing [ADR 0030](0030-where-movement-is-allowed.md) governs again, alongside the screen shift and the petals it kept governing throughout. Somebody who has turned animation off gets the next picture with no push, no bloom and no sun — the place is simply the next one — and a phone gets exactly the same transition everybody else does, because the rule that once answered for it belonged to the camera and left with it.

## What #151 already found, and this ticket confirms

**A picture cannot be magnified, which is exactly why one continuous world could not become three separate images by half-measures.** The original version of this record said as much as a warning against a future ticket: "a ticket that proposes drawing the hall as its own scene, cross-fading two pictures, or pre-rendering the world to a bitmap is answered by this record: the nesting is what makes one camera enough, and a bitmap cannot be magnified 3.6 times." That warning was correct about a bitmap of the _old_ world — a single flat render of the forest could not have stood in for the camera's zoom. It undersold the actual fix, which was not to bake the old geometry into a bitmap but to commission three separate paintings, each already composed at the distance its screens need, with the doorway's interior painted into the doors picture rather than magnified into it. The lesson generalises: a mechanism built for one representation does not have to survive being ported to another representation term for term, provided the new representation is given the chance to solve the same problem its own way.

**`gate-chrome.ts` already showed what "a place on a picture" means for the interface standing on it**, before this ticket touched the doors or the hall. The original camera positioned the gate's name at `lintel.y / 2`, a formula over the painted world's own geometry; #151 replaced it with fixed percentage bands measured against the real pixels of `gate.jpg`, because a photograph has no lintel to be half above. This ticket does not add equivalent chrome for the doors or the hall — neither has interface text pinned to a point of the artwork yet — but the pattern is established for whoever's ticket does: measure the shipped picture, do not compute from a world that no longer exists.

## The camera, for the record

What follows describes a mechanism this application no longer has. It is kept because ADR 0001 asks decisions to be recorded rather than deleted, and because "why doesn't this project have a camera any more" deserves the same answer as "why does it": read on.

There was one camera for the whole application, and it never cut: no swapping of one picture for another, no dissolve between two scenes, and no second rendering of anything. The camera was a rectangle of one world, and a journey was that rectangle moving and shrinking; the painting modules never learned that anything was moving at all, because a frame of a journey was `paintScene` called with a frame that had changed a little.

Walking into the temple was a magnification, and that is what the doorway being a hole was for: the hall was painted inside the opening and clipped to it, in the opening's own 1440 by 900. The journey from the doors to the hall was the same arithmetic as the journey from the gate to the doors — the frame got smaller until it was smaller than the doorway, and at that point the whole window was indoors. Nothing was loaded, nothing was swapped, and there was no moment at which the outside was replaced by the inside.

Eleven hundred milliseconds on `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)`, with the magnification interpolated through its logarithm: smooth away, quickest in the middle, smooth in — one duration for every journey, however far it was. Starting the camera was a promise that the screen ahead was ready: once anything was moving, nothing could be fetched, measured, laid out, decoded or downloaded, and the interface went out over the first 22% of a journey and came back over the last 26%, so the middle of every journey was the place alone. A phone got no camera and the places changed at once; `prefers-reduced-motion` switched it off the same way.

The petals stopped being the reward once a finished game moved indoors, and a cloth took their place — that decision is unaffected by this rewrite and lives on in the "Decision" section above and in `garden/cloth.ts`, `garden/paint-cloth.ts` and `garden/RewardCloth.tsx`. The lobby stopped showing the crossword once the lobby became the temple doors, for the same reason: the middle of that screen was the opening the camera was about to fly through, and the crossword now stands in a game screen with a picture behind it instead.

## Consequences

- A scene is a picture, full stop. A ticket proposing to derive a screen's background from world coordinates, or to bring back a "location" with an `x`, `y` and `zoom`, is answered by this record: the world those coordinates described does not exist, and a picture cannot be computed from arithmetic that no longer has anything to point at
- `sceneFor` (`garden/use-room-garden.ts`) is the one place a room screen's picture is decided, and it has no `default` case for the same reason the old `locationFor` switch did not: an eighth screen is a decision about which picture it stands in front of, and the way to lose that decision is to let a switch fall through to whichever case happened to be written first
- The transition between two pictures was deliberately unbuilt here, and [#153](https://github.com/vik8174/word-crossword-game/issues/153) is where it was built: the crossfade, the push, the bloom and the sun described above, and the step [`../manual-checks.md`](../manual-checks.md) grows for it under "When movement was touched"
- The end of a game is still keyed on the change from a game to a finished game inside one session, never on the finished screen being shown ([0030](0030-where-movement-is-allowed.md)). That rule did not depend on the camera and does not change here
- Petals no longer avoid a doorway-shaped rectangle. If a future ticket regenerates the doors artwork without a painted glimpse of the hall inside it, or wants petals to visibly avoid that glimpse, the fix is a percentage band measured against that picture's real pixels — the way `gate-chrome.ts` already works — not a return of world coordinates
- Only the gate's picture is preloaded (`build/scene-preload.ts`, unchanged by #153): `doors.avif` and `hall.avif` are fetched the ordinary way, by the `<img>` that first asks for them, because which one a room needs depends on the room's own state rather than on the address alone — there is no path to gate a preload script on the way `/` gates the gate's. A first crossfade onto either picture on a cold cache therefore waits on a network fetch of up to about 160 KiB before it can begin, which neither this record nor #153 has to solve; it is a plain image load like any other on the page
