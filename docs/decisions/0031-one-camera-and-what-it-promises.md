# 0031. One camera, and what starting it promises

Status: Accepted

## Context

[#115](https://github.com/vik8174/word-crossword-game/issues/115) gave the app a place: one painted world 3200 by 1400, four locations in it, and every screen of a room standing at one of them ([0030](0030-where-movement-is-allowed.md) had already decided what may move and what may not). What it did not give the place was distance. A player at the gate who joined a room arrived at the temple doors between two frames, and a player whose game began was inside the hall before the sentence they were saying had finished. The geography was real and nobody travelled through it.

This is the ticket that adds the travelling, and three decisions in it are each one line of code away from being written the other way round — so they are recorded here rather than left in a ticket that will be closed.

Two more things arrived in `main` with #115 and had nowhere to be written down, because that ticket had no ADR of its own to write them in. They are consequences of the place being a place, they change what a player sees, and the second of them reverses a decision that is recorded in an issue everybody still reads. They are set down at the end of this record.

## Decision

**One camera for the whole application, and it never cuts.** There is no swapping of one picture for another, no dissolve between two scenes, and no second rendering of anything. The camera is a rectangle of the one world, and a journey is that rectangle moving and shrinking; the painting modules never learn that anything is moving at all, because a frame of a journey is `paintScene` called with a frame that has changed a little (`garden/camera.ts`, `garden/use-camera.ts`, `garden/SceneLayer.tsx`).

**Walking into the temple is a magnification, and that is what the doorway being a hole is for.** The hall is painted inside the opening and clipped to it, in the opening's own 1440 by 900 (`garden/world.ts`). So the journey from the doors to the hall is the same arithmetic as the journey from the gate to the doors: the frame gets smaller until it is smaller than the doorway, and at that point the whole window is indoors. Nothing is loaded, nothing is swapped, and there is no moment at which the outside is replaced by the inside. It also means the rule that keeps the weather outdoors — a petal whose place falls inside the doorway is not drawn — goes on holding while the camera is passing through the doorway, without a flag anywhere saying whether we have arrived.

The clearance is deliberate and is not to be tidied away: at a magnification of 3.6 the frame is 400 by 250 against a doorway of 460 by 290. Without the margin the corners of the window show the door frame at the end of the journey.

**Eleven hundred milliseconds on `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)`, with the magnification interpolated through its logarithm.** Smooth away, quickest in the middle, smooth in. This replaces the answer given during the first grilling — a sharp start and a long slowing down — which is right for a thing that leaves and wrong for a camera: at the gate the whole forest is on the screen, and a camera that leaves sharply takes the entire picture with it, which reads as a jolt rather than as departure.

The logarithm is not a detail. From the gate to the hall the magnification grows three and a half times over, and interpolated in a straight line it is past two thirds of the way in at the halfway mark — so the last part of the journey arrives in a rush nobody asked for. Through the logarithm, every equal part of the journey multiplies the magnification by the same factor, which is what approaching something at a steady rate means when the thing being changed is a magnification.

**One duration for every journey, however far it is.** What the four places are is a step of a game, and a step of a game takes as long as a step of a game takes, whatever distance they happen to be drawn apart.

**Starting the camera is a promise that the screen ahead is ready.** Once anything is moving, nothing may be fetched, measured, laid out, decoded or downloaded: the middle of a travelling frame is where a player is looking hardest, and a stutter there is the one thing this whole ticket could produce that is worse than not having it. The budget for getting ready is 140 milliseconds.

What makes the promise cheap is the order things already happen in, and that order is the decision. The screen changes **first** — the room's data arrives with a snapshot, `RoomScreen` becomes a different kind, React mounts the new screen and lays it out — and only then does the effect that follows say where the garden is standing, which is what starts the camera. Being ready is therefore not something anything has to remember to do; it is what has already happened by the time the camera exists. The garden is one canvas that does not load, so the React screen is the only thing there ever was to prepare.

Measured on the reference window, the preparation for the heaviest change in the app — a lobby becoming a game, which mounts a whole board — is a single frame of 59 milliseconds, and the longest frame after the camera starts is 26.

**The interface goes out over the first 22% of the journey and comes back over the last 26%**, so the middle of every journey is the place alone. It is one `opacity` on one element, set from inside the camera's own frame rather than rendered: a journey rendered sixty times a second would spend the frames it is made of. The fade goes quickly and then settles rather than running down in a straight line, and that is not taste either — the shift between two screens of a room is 180 milliseconds of its own and is not this ticket's to change ([#93](https://github.com/vik8174/word-crossword-game/issues/93)), so it plays underneath the first sixth of every journey. A straight line lets the screen it brings in be seen at about half strength and then taken away again, which is a flash of the destination at the moment of leaving.

**A phone gets no camera, and the places change at once.** The frame is anchored to the window's height and never to its width ([#115](https://github.com/vik8174/word-crossword-game/issues/115)), so a phone sees a narrow slice of the same painting. A slice of a journey is not a shorter journey; it is a stripe of green sliding past, and it is a second of it on the device least able to spare the frames. What counts as a phone is the question the room already answers about itself (`components/room-layout.ts`, `APP_SHELL`), asked once rather than twice with two answers.

**`prefers-reduced-motion` switches the camera off, and does not slow it down.** Off means the place changes between two frames and the interface is never taken off the screen on the way. It is the same answer the petals and the shift give, and there is no setting in the app that overrides it ([0030](0030-where-movement-is-allowed.md)).

**A game ending is not a journey.** The hall and the hall at the end of a game are two locations and one place, so the camera does not move at all when a game finishes: the player stays at the table they played at. Without this the camera would run its whole duration going nowhere and take the interface off the screen and back on the way, which is the opposite of what an ending is for. It falls out of the arithmetic rather than out of a special case — two places with the same point and the same magnification are the same place (`isSamePlace`).

## What #115 left behind, recorded here

**The petals stopped being the reward, and a cloth took their place.** [0030](0030-where-movement-is-allowed.md) decided that the greeting at the end of a game is the garden coming back: the petals that were absent all game start thickly and thin out. That decision was made when a finished game was a page with a sky behind it. Since #115 a finished game stands at `CONGRATULATIONS`, inside the temple's doorway, and the weather does not come indoors — so the sky the greeting asked for is not there, and between #115 and this ticket the end of a game showed nothing at all.

The answer is not to let the petals back in. The rule that keeps them out of the doorway is geometry rather than a flag, and it is the same rule the camera flies through the doorway on; an exception for one screen would be an exception in the middle of the mechanism this record is about.

So the greeting is now a cloth drawn across the table from both sides, made of the temple's own paper and the temple's own red (`garden/cloth.ts`, `garden/paint-cloth.ts`, `garden/RewardCloth.tsx`). It takes 1900 milliseconds; the two halves meet at 58% of that and the words follow from 64%, so the picture is given to the reader first and the sentence second. What is printed on it was printed before either half moved — the pattern is worked out once for the whole banner and the halves are windows onto it, which is why it runs through the seam without a join. The paper going on showing the room behind it is a decision too: the board they filled in is still there, put down rather than taken away.

The cloth is made of what the place is made of and not out of interface. An ending drawn as a card, a dialog or a colour off the theme would be the application congratulating somebody; what is wanted is the room they played in doing it. Its one action leaves for the gate, and leaving is what makes the camera ride back out through the doorway.

It belongs to the room rather than to the garden, and is mounted by `RoomPage`. Two reasons, and the second is the harder one: it is a screen of a finished game rather than weather over a place, and it is a reward nobody has yet earned on the landing page, so having it inside the route's own chunk is what keeps a first visit from paying for it ([#92](https://github.com/vik8174/word-crossword-game/issues/92), `build/first-visit-weight.ts`).

**The lobby no longer shows the crossword, and [#101](https://github.com/vik8174/word-crossword-game/issues/101) is reversed on that point.** #101 decided that the board stands in the lobby at the size it will be played at, "so nothing moves when the game starts". Since #115 the lobby is the temple doors, and the middle of that screen **is** the opening: on a 1440 by 900 window the doorway falls on x 283 to 1157 and the board occupied x 353 to 1087. A board there is a board standing in the doorway the camera is about to fly through.

So the lobby says what the crossword is in words and keeps everything in the left band, and the middle of the doors screen stays empty. This is a real loss and is accepted knowingly: the board now appears together with the game, which makes the journey into the hall responsible for something it was not responsible for before — the board arriving. That it arrives while the interface is still coming back over the last quarter of the journey is what keeps the arrival from being a snap.

## Consequences

- The camera is one transformation over the geometry of #115 and is not a second renderer. A ticket that proposes drawing the hall as its own scene, cross-fading two pictures, or pre-rendering the world to a bitmap is answered by this record: the nesting is what makes one camera enough, and a bitmap cannot be magnified 3.6 times
- Anything the app pins to a place in the world has to ask the camera where it is standing at the moment of drawing rather than be handed a location. The doorway moves and grows for the length of every journey, and a petal culled against the doorway as it stood a second ago is a petal missing out of the middle of the sky
- The order "screen first, camera second" is the requirement most easily lost. Anything that starts a journey before the screen ahead exists — a route fetched on the way, a location changed ahead of the data — breaks the promise without breaking a test, and what it produces is a stutter in the middle of a frame that is travelling
- The four places are the only values the camera moves between, and they are still a point and a magnification and nothing else. A fifth place is a decision about where in the world a screen happens, exactly as it was in #115
- A cost accepted knowingly: the frames in which a game becomes a finished game are the frames in which React unmounts a board and mounts a finished one, and they are longer than the frames of the cloth being drawn over them. On the reference window four frames in the first 373 milliseconds run between 33 and 51 milliseconds, and every frame of the 1.9 seconds after that is under 10. The cloth's own drawing is not what costs; the screen changing underneath it is, and that cost is the same one every screen change in this app has always had
- The end of a game is still keyed on the change from a game to a finished game inside one session, never on the finished screen being shown ([0030](0030-where-movement-is-allowed.md)). A completed room is terminal, so a cloth keyed on the screen would be laid again on every reload and in every tab opened an hour later. Somebody who opens a finished room cold is shown a calm result, and that is the answer rather than a gap
- The manual checks grow a section: what moves now cannot be asserted, only looked at ([`../manual-checks.md`](../manual-checks.md))
